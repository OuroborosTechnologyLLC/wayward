Introduction

There were prodigies and portents enough...

–Glen Cook, The Black Company, Chapter 1: Legate

The world ebbs and flows between order and chaos. Civilizations rise, conquer the chaos and wildernesses of the world, then become decadent and, inevitibly fall. Dark ages, golden ages, they matter little to those who wait for the right moment to strike.

Only the strongest and hardiest survive the waves of death and life that would suffocate and drag down the masses.Wayward!

One Basic Rule

Rule 0: The Referee is the final arbiter of all game-related questions.

Basic Rules

These rules cover dice notation and uncertain actions.

Die Roll Conventions

The game uses six-sided dice, written as d6. A number before “d6” is the number of dice rolled; add their results.

Example: 2d6 means roll two six-sided dice and add the results.

A modifier is added to or subtracted from a throw.

Example: 2d6-2 means roll two dice, add them, then subtract 2.

For a d66 throw, designate one die as tens and another as ones before rolling. This produces 36 results, from 11 to 66.

The Basic Mechanic

Throw 2d6: Roll 2d6 and add the relevant Skill. A total equal to or above the target succeeds; a lower total fails. The default target is 8+.

Skill notation: “Throw Skill 6+” means roll 2d6 + Skill against 6+. Without a listed target, use 8+. A listed target always takes precedence.

A natural 2 always fails and may be a critical fumble. A natural 12 always succeeds and is critical.

Referee Guidance

Throw only when the outcome is uncertain and both success and failure matter. Routine tasks succeed without a throw. Use 8+ or another suitable difficulty when danger makes a simple task uncertain.

An unlisted Skill is 0. Throw 2d6 without a Skill modifier.

Referee Guidance

Skill 0 represents resourcefulness, not universal knowledge. Raise the difficulty for tasks requiring unfamiliar knowledge or training. Allow no throw when success is implausible.

Example: An untrained character may recall a fact from a traveler’s tale, an old song, or practical experience.

Freedom of Action

Characters may attempt any plausible action. Archetypes and Combat Moves do not limit ordinary actions, but do add more potential and enhanced actions to a character's repetoire.

Referee Guidance

Players state their intent and approach. Referees should decide whether it succeeds, requires a throw, or is impossible. Impossibility must follow from the established situation, not a missing ability.

Describe perceptible difficulty and danger before a throw. Reveal exact targets or consequences only when the character could judge them. Players may attempt plausible actions but cannot demand particular odds or outcomes.

Example: “That jump looks difficult” may reveal all the character can judge; its exact target and damage may remain unknown.

Hidden Information

Sometimes, when the situation is unclear, information usually available to players is hidden. The Referee may describe what is available to the player from the perspective of the character, but not everything will, nor should, be easily accessible.

Referee Guidance

It is appropriate to conceal information the characters lack, including targets, resistance throws, damage, properties, abilities, and consequences. Roll secretly when the roll itself would reveal hidden information.

Describe only what each character can perceive and understand. Expertise may reveal more.

Example: A Healer may recognize impending death where another character sees only a grave wound.

It is permissible to be vague or mistaken but must not knowingly present false information as objective fact. Lies, rumors, illusions, and errors must come through the game world.

Consistent Rulings

The rules herein do not cover every situation that may occur in a game or at a table. When this happens, the Referee and players may work together in creating a consistent rule.

Referee Guidance

Rule similar situations alike. Treat established rulings as precedent unless circumstances or clearer rules change them. Examples illustrate; they do not limit play.

Failure Changes the Situation

Failure changes the situation: it may cost time or supplies, attract attention, damage equipment, strain relations, create danger, or force a choice.

However, the same throw cannot be attempted unless permitted by the Referee or the situation substantially changes.

Referee Guidance

Do not permit a repeated but an unchanged attempt. A new throw requires a new approach, changed circumstances, or new risk.

Task Difficulties

The standard target is 8+. Use the table for easier or harder tasks. Each 2-point target change equals the opposite modifier.

Example: 6+ target equals 8+ target with modifier +2; 10+ target equals 8+ target with modifier −2.

Referee Guidance

Judge the whole situation instead of adding minor modifiers. Tools, help, time, visibility, footing, and similar factors determine whether the action is automatic, impossible, or uncertain; its difficulty; and its consequences.

Time and Haste

For tasks affected by care or speed, the Referee sets the normal time and difficulty. The player chooses a pace before rolling.

Each doubling of time makes the task one step easier (-2 to the target). Each halving makes it one step harder (+2). Minimum Easy 6+; maximum Formidable 14+. A faster attempt beyond 14+ is impossible.

Characters

Rules for character creation and advancement.

Character Creation

  1. Choose a character concept.
  2. Distribute 5 points among the six Skills. No Skill may begin above 3.
  3. Choose one Archetype: the character’s training, former occupation, or social position.
  4. If Combat is 2+, choose one Combat Move per level above 1. Meet each Move’s minimum Combat rating.
  5. Record Stamina equal to 14 + three times Physical, Defense equal to 8 + half Combat (rounded up), and Resolve 0.
  6. Mark Connection Slots equal to 3 + Social on the character sheet. Review Patrons and Contacts in the Gameplay and Exploration chapter later.
  7. Begin with food and water for 3 days, a backpack, a waterskin, and a knife. Choose additional gear using the Starting Equipment rules described later in the chapter. Begin at Wealth Tier 2 (Poor) with 1 WP.
  8. If Lore is 1+, choose one spell per Lore level. Sorcerers gain their Archetype’s additional spells.
  9. Choose a name and appearance. Briefly describe why the character left their former life and what now draws them toward danger.

Skills

The six Skills are Combat, Craft, Lore, Physical, Social, and Stealth.

Combat: violence with any weapon, from fists to catapults.

Craft: making, repairing, practical knowledge, and wilderness survival.

Lore: formal learning, medicine, history, alchemy, the occult, and spellcasting.

Physical: exertion, athletics, and resistance to bodily harm such as poison.

Social: bargaining, lying, carousing, and negotiation.

Stealth: sneaking, pickpocketing, lockpicking, and other subterfuge.

Demi-Humans

Some settings permit demi-human characters. Unlike humans, demi-humans have only 4 points to distribute between their Skills during character creation rather than 5. They otherwise choose an Archetype and receive Combat Moves normally, in addition to their species’ special abilities.

Antediluvians: sorcerous humanoids predating extant civilization by millennia. They can sense magic instinctively within a 30m radius, without requiring a throw (see the Sorcery chapter for details). When they encounter demons, sentient undead, and other Chaotic beings, improve the resulting Reaction by one level.

Apefolk: Apes uplifted by sorcery or super-science to full sentience. Ordinary feats of brute strength succeed without a throw, and they may attempt extraordinary feats of strength that would be impossible for a human. Their Stamina is calculated from Physical normally.

Dwarf: Short, sturdy subterranean folk, often smiths or masons. Ordinary poison and disease are resisted with modifier +2; supernatural or virulent effects are resisted with modifier +1. Underground or in mountains, throw Craft 6+ to spot secret doors, traps, or abnormal construction.

Elf: Forest spirit-folk who live long lives and are prone to melancholy. May hide completely in minimal natural cover, and can sneak around in natural settings with modifier +2 on Stealth throws. They see in dim light as daylight and in darkness as dim light.

Gecko-Kin: Gecko humanoids who climb walls and ceilings as easily as level ground. They see in dim light as daylight and in darkness as dim light.

Lizard-Kin: Aquatic lizard humanoids. Their unarmed damage is 2d6, natural Armor is 2, and they can hold their breath for 10 minutes.

Archetypes

An Archetype describes a character’s former life and experience. It grants no numerical bonus. It establishes:

  • Knowledge: ordinary facts the character knows.
  • Competence: routine work requiring no throw under suitable conditions.
  • Possibility: specialized actions an outsider could not attempt.
  • Connections: relevant people, institutions, duties, and rivalries.

An Archetype does not dictate personality. On an uncertain task, it changes what is possible or at stake, not the throw. A character without the relevant Archetype may need instruction, equipment, information, or another approach. The descriptions are not exhaustive.

Examples: A Burglar can open a difficult lock but may throw to finish before a patrol arrives. A Sage can interpret a damaged manuscript but may throw to determine how much survives.

Archetypes that do not fit the setting, or whose names would be better if changed, may be done so. But next to the altered name, in parantheses add the functional Archetype.

Example: Gladiator (Warrior)

List of Archetypes

Beast Master

You understand animal behavior, feeding, breeding, injury, and distress. With time and materials, you can care for, restrain, and train domesticated animals without a throw.

You may calm, train, or convey simple intentions to wild or dangerous animals when their nature permits. You may know handlers, herders, hunters, stablekeepers, or remote communities.

Berserker

You understand warrior customs, weapons, boasts, feuds, and battle fury. You can distinguish ritual violence, intimidation, and intent to kill.

Once per battle, after losing Stamina but remaining conscious, you may enter a Rage. During it, ignore fear and penalties from pain or Wounds. You cannot cast spells, retreat voluntarily, or perform precise work. Rage ends when no immediate enemy remains, you fall unconscious, or an ally spends an action calming you. Deferred penalties then apply.

Burglar

You understand locks, traps, alarms, concealed entrances, guarded valuables, pockets, purses, and methods of hiding stolen goods. With tools and time, you can bypass ordinary security, case a commonplace building, or palm an unattended object without a throw.

You may attempt expert lockwork, disarm unusual traps, enter guarded structures, pick pockets, or perform other difficult acts of physical theft. You may know locksmiths, servants, lookouts, second-story crews, or buyers of stolen goods.

Deulist

You understand dueling customs, challenges, footwork, weapon care, and fighting schools. You can identify an observed fencer’s skill and school when evidence permits.

You may seek recognition, hospitality, instruction, or formal challenge from other duelists. At the Referee's discretion, schools bring rivals, debts, reputations, teachings, and duties.

Explorer

You understand maps, camps, weather, landmarks, hazards, and expeditions. In ordinary conditions, you can camp, preserve supplies, and follow a usable map without a throw.

You may navigate unmapped country, reconstruct damaged routes, or organize travel through extreme environments. You may know guides, caravan leaders, frontier settlements, scholars, or other explorers.

Healer

You understand wounds, disease, childbirth, sanitation, medicine, and signs of death. With materials and time, you can treat ordinary injuries and ailments without a throw.

You may perform Triage, surgery, difficult diagnoses, and other specialized care. Throws determine speed, complications, supply use, or survival. You may know physicians, midwives, apothecaries, hospices, or temples.

Hunter

You understand tracks, animals, traps, weather, camouflage, and wilderness travel. Where resources exist, you can find food and shelter, identify common animals, and follow an open trail without a throw.

Scarcity, haste, dangerous quarry, hidden tracks, or hostile conditions require a throw. You may know guides, trappers, isolated settlements, local paths, or predators.

Knight

You understand heraldry, noble customs, horses, armor, fortifications, military duties, and warrior etiquette.

You can care for mounts and arms without a throw. Where your station is recognized, you may claim an audience, hospitality, or formal challenge, but also face duties and enemies. Start with the Armor Training Combat Move.

Mariner

You understand sails, ropes, weather, watches, cargo, discipline, coasts, and dangerous water. In ordinary weather, you can work aboard ship and navigate familiar waters without a throw.

You may attempt difficult navigation, emergency repairs, command, or handling a vessel in danger. You may know ports, pilots, shipowners, smugglers, fishermen, or distant customs.

Merchant

You understand markets, contracts, measures, trade routes, prices, credit, profit, and risk. You can assess familiar goods without a throw.

You may find markets, arrange transport, detect fraud, and negotiate unfamiliar or sensitive trades. You may know guilds, caravans, moneylenders, factors, or officials; and such ties, subject to the Referee, may require obligations.

Pilgrim

You understand roads, trails, city gates, cheap lodging, camps, weather, and travelers’ customs. In ordinary conditions, you can navigate settlements, blend into crowds, and camp with little or no gear.

You may cross unfamiliar cities unnoticed or travel settled or wild country with scant supplies. You may know shrines, hostels, ferries, caravan stops, back roads, or travelers.

Priest

You understand your religion’s rites, scriptures, hierarchy, calendar, laws, saints, enemies, and disputes. You can conduct ordinary ceremonies and recognize its symbols without a throw.

Begin with three divine spells. Lore 1 is required to cast divine spells or learn more. Whenever Lore grants a spell, you may choose it from the Divine Spells list or the general Spells list.

Where your faith has influence, you may request hospitality, records, aid, or an audience. Its members may impose duties or question your conduct.

Pugilist

You understand unarmed contests, holds, training, crowds, injuries, and fighting houses. Your unarmed attacks inflict 2d6 damage instead of 1d6.

You may identify fighting styles, enter fighting venues, and find trainers, gamblers, promoters, or rivals.

Rogue

You understand confidence tricks, disguises, forgery, bribery, smuggling, blackmail, gambling, illicit markets, criminal signals, and underworld hierarchies. In familiar criminal surroundings, you can observe local etiquette, recognize common schemes, and locate routine illicit goods or services without a throw.

You may attempt elaborate cons, maintain false identities, forge convincing documents, move contraband, compromise officials, or negotiate between criminal groups. You may know fences, smugglers, fixers, informants, corrupt officials, gangs, or people who owe dangerous favors.

Runner

You understand pacing, routes, fatigue, footwear, obstacles, messages, and relays. You can judge sustainable speed and maintain an ordinary courier’s pace without a throw.

You may attempt extreme journeys, dangerous climbs, rapid crossings, or delivery through hostile territory. You may know roads, shortcuts, relay stations, messengers, scouts, or information brokers.

Sage

Choose one scholarly field. You know its common facts, methods, authorities, disputes, and language. You can recall ordinary information and use its tools without a throw.

You may interpret obscure sources, conduct original research, and locate answers. You may know libraries, schools, patrons, rivals, collectors, or forbidden archives.

Examples: alchemy, astronomy, history, law, mathematics, natural philosophy, or theology.

Shaman

You understand your tradition’s spirits, ancestors, dreams, taboos, sacred places, rites, stories, and duties. You can conduct its ordinary ceremonies and recognize spiritual disturbance without a throw.

Once per week, perform a Divine Audience without a Lore throw or mishap. You may know spirit keepers, clans, or sacred communities. Spirits retain their own intentions.

Skirmisher

You understand observation, concealment, range, firing ground, missile trajectories, fields of fire, and ambushes.

With time and suitable terrain, you can prepare a concealed firing position and judge range without a throw. You may recognize another marksman’s methods or reconstruct a shot.

Sorcerer

You understand magical terms, rituals, grimoires, and sorcerous traditions. You can identify common ritual materials and obvious spellcraft without a throw.

Begin with three additional spells from the general Spells lists. Lore 1 is required to cast them or learn more. You may interpret unfamiliar magic, seek occult instruction, or identify a working’s tradition. You may have rivals, creditors, obligations, or a demanding master.

Warrior

You understand weapons, armor, formations, watches, camps, supply, morale, customs, and trained fighters. You can assess arms, organize a military camp, and drill willing combatants without a throw.

You may recognize tactics, negotiate with military groups, and claim other warriors’ regard or hostility. Gain two additional Combat Moves for which you qualify.

Professional

A catch-all Archetype. Choose a noncombat profession not covered elsewhere. Define its knowledge, routine work, specialized tasks, tools, and community with the Referee. It must matter during adventures without covering general adventuring. It grants no numerical bonus.

Examples: advocate, architect, cook, courier, farmer, herald, miner, scribe, steward, or undertaker.

Optional Rule: Only Sorcerers Can Use Sorcery

Optionally, only a character with the Sorcerer Archetype and Lore 1+ may learn or cast spells from the general Spells lists. Characters with other Archetypes cannot choose general spells during character creation, gain them from Lore, or learn them from tomes. A Priest with Lore 1+ may still learn and cast divine spells.

This represents the difficult nature of spellcasting, magic, and the occult. Such characters are rare and specialized, and have dedicated years to studying sorcery.

Optional Rule: Seasoned Characters ("The Cadfael Rule")

Optionally, Referees may permit a character to take on a second Archetype at creation. Reduce maximum Stamina by 3, and treat Physical throws against Fatigue, disease, deprivation, and prolonged exertion as one difficultly level harder.

The character may not improve Physical, but may still gain a third Archetype.

Named in homage to Brother Cadfael, created by Ellis Peters. Cadfael is a monk who, when younger, participated in the First Crusade. He would have been considered a Warrior/Healer or Warrior/Priest character.

Combat Moves

Combat Moves are practiced techniques, not limits on ordinary actions or cumulative bonuses.

Gain one Move per Combat level above 1. A Move requires the Combat rating under which it appears. This also applies to Moves gained from Warrior or advancement.

Use a Move whenever its stated conditions apply. A reaction or interrupt consumes the character’s next available Combat Action. The same future action cannot be spent twice.

Combat 2 Moves

Armor Training: Ignore heavy armor’s movement penalty. You may climb, mount, and perform similar ordinary actions in it. Extreme swimming, stealth, heat, and endurance remain subject to circumstances.

Disarm: On a successful disarming stunt, inflict normal damage and disarm the target. Skilled combatants may resist by throwing Physical 8+.

Guardian: Once per round, when an adjacent ally is chosen as the sole target of an attack, become the target before the attack is resolved if you can interpose yourself. This reaction consumes your next Combat Action. Use your Defense and Armor. Area attacks are unaffected.

Quick: When your side is Surprised, take one Combat Action during the first round before the Surprising side takes their turn. Other Surprise effects still apply.

Set Against Charge: When an enemy moves at least 5m directly toward you before a melee attack, attack first with a braced spear or similar weapon. This reaction consumes your next Combat Action. If you incapacitate, knock down, or drive back the enemy, its attack fails.

Shield Bash: With a shield, attempt to knock down or drive back a creature of roughly your size. On a hit, inflict normal unarmed damage and complete the stunt. Skilled combatants may resist by throwing Physical 8+.

Study Opponent: Spend one Combat Action observing a visible opponent. Ask one question about its apparent training, tactics, weapons, defenses, injuries, or intent. The Referee answers what observation can reveal. Hidden qualities require visible evidence.

Combat 3 Moves

Defend Others: Spend one Combat Action protecting an adjacent ally until the start of your next turn. While you remain adjacent and able to act, attacks that target only that ally suffer modifier −2. The ally remains the target and uses their own Defense and Armor. Area attacks are unaffected.

Mounted Charge: After moving at least 10m on a controlled mount, a melee hit inflicts normal damage and drives back or knocks down a target that is not too large or firmly positioned. Skilled combatants may resist by throwing Physical 8+.

Pinning Shot: Before a ranged attack, name clothing, equipment, loose armor, or nearby material that could pin the target. On a hit, inflict normal damage and pin it. It may spend an action escaping or abandoning the pinned item.

Rage: Once per battle, after losing Stamina but remaining conscious, enter a Berserker’s Rage. A Berserker with this Move may instead enter Rage at the start of their turn.

Retaliation: Once per round, after an enemy hits you with a melee attack and its damage is resolved, make one melee attack against it if you remain conscious and it remains within reach. This attack must inflict damage rather than attempt a stunt. This reaction consumes your next Combat Action.

Riposte: Once per round, after an enemy misses you with a melee attack, exploit the opening to make one melee attack or attempt a stunt against it if it remains within reach. This reaction consumes your next Combat Action.

Rally: After a Morale roll is thrown for your side, spend one Combat Action (or next Combat Action if out of actions) addressing allies within 10m who can hear and understand you. Each may repeat one Morale check failed during this battle. The new result stands. Rally applies once per failed check.

Combat 4 Moves

Hold the Line: While conscious and able to fight, enemies cannot pass through your melee reach without driving you back, incapacitating you, or avoiding your reach. They may withdraw or attack normally.

Interrupt: Once per round, after another combatant declares an action but before it resolves, take one Combat Action first. The declared action then proceeds if possible. This cannot interrupt another Interrupt and consumes your next Combat Action.

Masterful Stunt: A successful ordinary stunt may also inflict half the attack’s rolled damage. Moves granting full damage still take precedence.

Whirlwind: Spend both Combat Actions to attack each enemy within melee reach once. Roll separately. You cannot move between attacks or combine Whirlwind with FRENZY!, Retaliation, or other additional attacks.

Combat 5 Moves

Command the Field: Spend one Combat Action ordering an ally within 10m who can hear and understand you. The ally immediately moves or takes one Combat Action without reducing their own turn’s actions. Each character may obey one such command per round.

Stronghold: While conscious, armed, and aware of your enemies, creatures of roughly your size cannot drive you back, knock you down, disarm you, or move you unwillingly. Once per round, sacrifice your next Combat Action to turn a melee hit into a miss. Massive creatures or overwhelming force may bypass this protection.

Wealth Rolls

When a transaction tests a character’s resources, make a Wealth Roll: throw Craft 8+ or Social 8+ according to the method:

  • Craft: locating suppliers, judging value, managing assets, organizing a large purchase, investing, or using formal credit.
  • Social: bargaining, bribery, invoking rank or reputation, arranging an informal loan, or persuading a seller to accept unusual terms.

Scarcity, urgency, hostile markets, damaged credit, or favorable opportunities may modify the throw.

Making Purchases

Compare the purchase’s Item Tier with the buyer’s Wealth Tier. Listed Tiers assume one ordinary item. Bulk, exceptional, illegal, or unique purchases may have a higher Tier or extra WP cost.

Trivial Purchases

An available item at least two Tiers below the buyer’s Wealth Tier requires no throw or WP.

Cheap Purchases

For an item one Tier below, make a Wealth Roll. Success acquires it without losing WP. After failure, wait 2d6 days before trying again.

Normal Purchases

For an item of equal Tier, make a Wealth Roll. Success acquires it for 1 WP. After failure, abandon the purchase or spend 2 WP to force it through. This requires sufficient WP.

Expensive Purchases

For an item above the buyer’s Tier, raise the Wealth Roll target by 2 per Tier of difference.

Success acquires the asset. Halve current WP, rounded down, or reduce it to the minimum for the next-lower Wealth Tier. After failure, wait one month before trying again. Tier 1 characters must pool resources or gain WP first.

Example: A Tier 2 character buying a Tier 4 warhorse throws Craft or Social 12+.

Changes in Wealth

Meeting a Tier’s WP requirement raises Wealth to that Tier. Wealth cannot rise again for one month. If WP falls below its requirement, Wealth immediately falls to the highest qualified Tier.

Living Below Your Means

Living at least one Tier below Wealth for a full month gains 1 WP.

Pooling Resources

Characters may pool WP for a shared asset. Determine Wealth Tier from the total. One assistant may throw Craft or Social 8+; success lowers the buyer’s difficulty one step. Other participants add resources but no modifier.

After failure, every participant suffers -1 to Wealth Rolls for one month.

Windfalls

Major rewards grant the WP listed in the treasure table. Bank them or spend one newly gained WP on one item at or below current Wealth Tier without a roll.

Timeframes

Purchases take reasonable time: hours or days for common gear, weeks for estates or large shipments, and months for ships, fortifications, or armies.

Status and Influence

Living above or below one’s Wealth Tier may attract suspicion, thieves, creditors, or taxes and modify Social throws where appearances matter.

Equipment and services: Listed entries are commonly traded in cities and towns. Villages may offer less.

Poisons

Poison families cover venoms, toxic plants, spores, minerals, and refined compounds. Setting-specific poisons use the closest family.

Example: Duskcap Dust may use the Fungal Poison rules. Arsenic would be considered a mineral poison.

Poison Pressure

Each poison has seven characteristics:

  • Tier: Wealth Tier (availability and cost).
  • Route: blood, breathed, skin, or swallowed.
  • Pressure: starting severity, from 1 to 4.
  • Onset: delay before the first test.
  • Frequency: time between later tests.
  • Symptom: effect of a failed test.
  • Crisis: effect at Pressure 6.

After exposure through the listed Route, record starting Pressure. At the end of Onset and each Frequency thereafter, throw Physical 8+.

  • On success, reduce Poison Pressure by 1.
  • On failure, resolve the poison’s Symptom and then increase Poison Pressure by 1.
  • A natural 12 reduces Poison Pressure by 2 instead of 1.
  • A natural 2 increases Poison Pressure by 2 instead of 1 after resolving the Symptom.
  • At Pressure 0, the poison passes; stop testing.
  • At Pressure 6, immediately resolve the poison’s Crisis. The Crisis entry states whether Pressure then falls, remains, or disappears.
  • Another dose before recovery adds starting Pressure to current Pressure, maximum 6. Resolve a Crisis immediately at 6.

Armor does not reduce poison damage. Unless stated otherwise, temporary Symptoms last until the next test. Damage and Wounds remain after recovery.

Track each family separately. Similar poisons in one family may count as the same poison.

Routes and Application

  • Blood: a contaminated source must cause Stamina damage.

  • Breathed: the victim must inhale it.

  • Skin: it must contact bare skin.

  • Swallowed: it must be eaten or drunk.

Applying one dose to a weapon takes one minute. It lasts eight hours or until the weapon causes Stamina damage. A dose may instead contaminate one serving of food or drink, or—if Breathed—one small enclosed room.

Suitable protection may prevent exposure.

Examples: A sealed mask may prevent Breathed exposure; thick gloves may prevent Skin exposure.

Recognition and Investigation

Throw Lore 8+ to examine suspected poison. Success identifies its family and likely Route. An exact formula, origin, or maker requires a sample, tools, and investigation.

Stealth may notice tampering; Craft may identify preparation. Discovery does not neutralize exposure.

Treatment

Before Onset ends, spend one Combat Action in an emergency or one minute otherwise to decontaminate, then throw Craft 8+. Success reduces starting Pressure by 1. Each exposure allows one attempt.

A prepared counteragent must match the family. Administering it takes one Combat Action, reduces current Pressure by 2, and doubles the next Frequency. Each exposure benefits from one counteragent.

A successful Sovereign Remedy removes an ordinary poison but not its damage or Wounds.

Examples of decontamination: wash skin, leave contaminated air, clean a wound, or empty the stomach.

Formulation

Manufacture requires a laboratory, a known formula, days equal to Tier, a Wealth Roll at that Tier (if materials are not already readily available), then throw Craft 8+.

Success produces 1 + Craft doses, minimum 1. Failure produces none. A Refined Alchemical Poison formula has one Route; another Route requires another formula.

A counteragent uses the same procedure and produces two applications. Learning one may require a sample of its poison family.

Creating Additional Poison Families

Use existing families as benchmarks:

  • Tier 1 poisons are irritating, conspicuous, or unlikely to cause lasting harm.
  • Tier 2 poisons are slow, unreliable, or principally incapacitating.
  • Tier 3 poisons are dangerous enough to kill an unprepared adventurer; their Crises are usually fatal.
  • Tier 4 poisons are rapid, difficult to acquire, and lethal at Crisis.
  • Tier 5 should be reserved for unique supernatural toxins, major quest ingredients, or formulas capable of threatening exceptionally powerful creatures.

More Routes, shorter Onset or Frequency, higher Pressure, unavoidable damage, or a worse Crisis raises Tier. Give each family one identity; do not combine several families’ strengths.

Combat

Combat is divided into 6-second rounds. A round covers everyone’s actions; a turn is one combatant’s opportunity to act within that round. In Initiative order, each combatant normally takes one turn and two Combat Actions. After all combatants have taken their turns, a new round begins. The Referee checks NPC Morale after resolving Surprise and whenever another Morale trigger occurs. Do not roll Triage when a character is first Wounded; after combat ends, resolve the 10-minute rest and make one Triage roll for each Wounded character.

Resolve combat in this order:

  • If needed, check Surprise: each group’s lowest Stealth throws Stealth 6+.

  • Each combatant rolls Initiative: 2d6 + Combat.

  • Each combatant may take two Combat Actions per turn (once per round) in Initiative order:

    • Attack (once per round unless stated otherwise)

    • Move

    • Help

    • Ready

    • Use an item

    • Take another brief action

  • When attacking:

    • Throw Combat against Defense.

    • On a hit, roll damage and subtract Armor.

    • At 0 Stamina, the target is Wounded and usually out of combat.

    • To keep fighting after a Wound, throw Physical 6+.

    • Each Wound beyond the first gives -1 to Triage and later keep-fighting throws.

    • Damage exceeding twice maximum Stamina kills instantly.

    • A natural 12 or a hit exceeding Defense by 6+ is critical and causes maximum damage.

    • Taking down a target triggers FRENZY!.

  • After two actions, that combatant's turn ends and the next combatant's turn begins. After all turns, begin a new round.

  • Referee checks Morale when its triggers apply.

  • After combat, resolve Healing and Triage.

Tip: Rolling attack and damage dice together speeds play.

Optional Combat Ranges

Range bands are rough shorthand. Printed meter values take precedence.

Conversion: 2m is Close; 10m, Near; 20–50m, Far; beyond 50m, Distant. Each 10m of movement is about one Near band. Weapon ranges 5/10m become Close/Near; 10/20m, Near/Far; 50/150m, Far/Distant.

  • Close: melee range, up to 2m. Move within it freely.

  • Near: thrown range or one Move Action.

  • Far: long thrown or effective bow range; 2–5 Move Actions.

  • Distant: difficult long-range shots; normally unreachable during combat.

Leaving Close range from a ready melee opponent grants it a free attack. Spend one Combat Action to disengage and avoid this.

Melee weapons attack at Close; weapons with reach may attack at Near. Convert Effective and Maximum ranges separately. Attack normally within Effective, at -2 up to Maximum, and not at all beyond Maximum unless a rule permits it.

Procedure

Surprise: Each party’s lowest-Stealth member throws Stealth 6+. If one succeeds and the other fails, the successful party gains one free round. Otherwise, neither is surprised. Apply +3 for an ambush; -1 for vehicles or more than 10 creatures.

Initiative: Each combatant rolls 2d6 + Combat. Act from highest to lowest.

Time and actions: A round is 6 seconds. Each combatant takes one turn with two Combat Actions. An action attacks, moves 10m, uses an item, or performs another brief act. Only one attack is allowed per round unless stated otherwise.

Help: Spend one Combat Action helping a non-attack action. Practical help may enable it, lower difficulty one step, reduce time, or soften failure. Helpers describe their contributions before the throw; benefits do not stack.

Ready: Delay one unspent Combat Action by stating an observable trigger and response. After the trigger resolves, take the readied action. Ready cannot interrupt. It expires at the start of your next turn. Normal restrictions apply.

Example: “When someone rounds the corner, I attack them.” The newcomer finishes rounding the corner before the attack.

Additional attacks: Use only one Move or feature granting extra attacks per round unless stated otherwise. Choose before attacking. FRENZY! uses its own limit but cannot combine with Whirlwind.

Interrupts: A character needs a rule permitting an interrupt and may use one per round. When it consumes the next Combat Action, deduct that action from the current turn or, if none remain, the next turn.

Negating harm: Apply one ability that reduces, negates, or transfers damage to each harmful effect. The player chooses.

Attacking: Throw Combat against Defense. Success hits. Attacks beyond Effective range suffer -2.

FRENZY! After taking an enemy out of combat in melee, move up to 2m and attack another foe in range. Repeat up to Combat times per round.

Damage: Roll weapon damage, subtract Armor, and reduce Stamina by the remainder. At 0 Stamina, the target is Wounded and usually out of combat. Throw Physical 6+ to keep fighting after receiving a Wound.

Further damage causes another Wound and another Physical 6+ throw. Each Wound beyond the first gives -1 to Triage and later keep-fighting throws.

One attack causing more than twice maximum Stamina in damage kills instantly.

Critical Hits: A natural 12, or exceeding Defense by 6+, causes maximum damage and any listed critical effect. A natural 12 always hits.

Healing: After combat, 10 minutes’ rest restores all positive Stamina. Each Wounded character rolls once on Triage: -1 per Wound beyond the first and -2 without first aid.

Morale: The Referee checks NPC Morale after their first death, Surprise, over 50% casualties, or severe suffering. Each side rolls 2d6 + its leader’s Social; -2 if the NPC leader is dead. Combat Stunts

Any character may attempt a plausible stunt instead of causing damage.

Declare the stunt before attacking. The Referee reveals obvious obstacles or required preparation but may conceal hidden defenses.

Make the attack normally. On a hit, choose one:

  • inflict the attack's normal damage; or
  • accomplish the declared stunt without inflicting damage.

Important or powerful opponents may resist with an appropriate Skill throw, normally Physical 8+. The Referee may roll secretly. A stunt cannot exceed what the attack could plausibly cause.

Combat Moves may add damage, alter timing, affect more targets, or exceed another limit. A character without a Move may still attempt its underlying stunt.

Examples: disarm, drive back, knock down, pin, distract, blind, restrain, intimidate, or seize an object. A knife cannot push a firmly planted giant; a pebble cannot pin an armored knight to a wall.

Sorcery

Rules for learning and casting dangerous magic.

Reading and Sensing Magic

A character with Lore may throw Lore 8+ to sense nearby magic or read magical inscriptions.

Learning New Spells

A character with Lore 1+ may learn a general spell from a tome. Each attempt takes one week and requires Lore 8+. A natural 2 destroys that spell’s formula, not the tome. A natural 2 or modified total of 4 or less also causes a Mishap.

Spellcasting

A character with Lore 1+ may cast a known spell from the general Spells lists. There is no daily limit. Throw Lore 8+.

Casting takes 10 minutes. Hasty casting takes one combat round and suffers modifier −2. A natural 12 always succeeds.

On success, the spell takes effect at the end of its 10-minute turn or combat round, after all other actions that round.

Each spell lists its range after its name.

Example: Respite (range: touch) requires touching the target.

Divine Magic

Only a character with the Priest Archetype and Lore 1+ may learn or cast divine spells unless another rule explicitly grants access. A Priest may learn a divine spell from scripture, revelation, or a religious instructor. Each attempt takes one week and requires Lore 8+. Failure has no further consequence: it never destroys scripture or causes a Mishap.

Divine spells are prayers. A Priest may attempt a number of prayers per day equal to Lore. Each attempt expends one prayer, whether it succeeds or fails; expended prayers return after a full night’s rest. Use the normal casting time, hasty casting, range, and Lore 8+ throw. A failed prayer simply has no effect. Divine magic never critically fails or causes a Mishap.

A prayer must uphold the faith’s established tenets: protect its faithful or the innocent, fulfill a religious duty, or oppose a recognized enemy. A harmful divine spell can affect only undead, demons, hostile spirits, or a mortal whom the faith’s established doctrine recognizes as an enemy or heretic.

Referee Guidance

Decide whether a prayer serves the faith’s established doctrine and tell the player before any prayer is expended. A Priest cannot declare someone an enemy or heretic merely to make a spell permissible.

Under the optional rule restricting sorcery to Sorcerers, a Priest may still cast divine spells. The Priest cannot cast spells from the general Spells lists unless they also have the Sorcerer Archetype.

Gameplay and Exploration

Rules for travel, exploration, Horror, and Connections.

Fatigue

A Fatigued character suffers -1 to all actions and moves at most 10m per round. Fatigue is separate from Stamina.

Overland Map and Movement

Wilderness maps normally use 10km (6-mile) hexes. On open plains, a character travels 30km (3 hexes) per day on foot. Encumbrance or heavy armor halves this.

Apply the region’s most appropriate terrain multiplier; multipliers do not stack.

Terrain Movement Effects

Terrain Movement Rate Multiplier
City/Urban 2/3
Desert/Badlands 2/3
Forest/Jungle 2/3
Hills/Mountains 1/3
Plains/Grasslands Full
Swamp/Aquatic 1/3

A trail grants full speed on foot or mounted, but not in vehicles. A road grants vehicles full speed.

Forced Marching

A forced march doubles one day’s range. At day’s end, throw Physical 4+ or become Fatigued; apply -1 per consecutive forced-march day after the first. Fatigued characters cannot force march.

Becoming Lost

A party following a trail, road, coast, or waterway cannot get lost. Otherwise, make the listed Craft throw each day. Bad weather gives -1; darkness gives -2. A natural 2 always fails.

Terrain Craft throw to avoid getting lost
City/Urban 4+
Desert/Badlands 6+
Forest/Jungle 6+
Hills/Mountains 4+
Open Seas 6+
Plains/Grasslands 3+
Swamps/Aquatic 5+

Camping

The party may make one throw to establish a defensible camp and avoid nightly random encounters: normally Craft 6+ in the wilderness, or Physical 6+ to reach a safe, inaccessible site.

Only characters on watch begin trouble alert and fully armored; others sleep in normal clothes. Each character must sleep at least half the night to gain rest benefits and avoid Fatigue.

Foraging

Foragers move at 2/3 speed after terrain effects and cannot force march. The party’s highest-Craft forager throws Craft 6+ to find one day’s food and water for all. Two or more helpers lower difficulty one step; others may find more or cover more ground. Adjust difficulty for terrain.

After 2 days without food, become Fatigued; after 1 week, suffer -2 to all actions; after 4 weeks, die.

Underground Movement

Dungeon maps normally use 3m (10ft) squares. Exploration uses 10-minute turns, each equal to 100 combat rounds. Careful exploration covers 120m per turn.

After 50 minutes exploring, rest for 1 turn or become Fatigued.

Light and Darkness

A torch casts dim light in a 10m radius. Dim light gives -1 to sight-based actions; darkness gives -2.

Horror and Resolve

Dungeons and decrepid castles usually hold something—whether natural or supernatural—that can stop adventurers cold. Horror violates nature or reality. Direct confrontation may require a Horror roll.

Fear comes from understandable danger. Players decide their characters’ response; NPCs use Morale. Rules that compel fear still apply.

Fear: a charging beast or collapsing bridge. Horror: purposeful undead, impossible transformation, alien geometry, or an incomprehensible entity.

Referee Guidance

Throw only when the Horror of a Monster is listed, when something terrible has been confronted, or when it is effective to the story or adventure.

It is best to use this sparingly.

Resolve

Resolve measures resistance to the unnatural. Ordinary people and new characters begin at 0. Add Resolve to Horror rolls. It is not a Skill and cannot be bought with XP. Minimum -5.

Horror Rolls

Throw 2d6 + Resolve + Horror modifier against 8+. Use only the worst modifier; they do not stack. The Referee may conceal it.

Horror ModifierExamples
0An inhuman but comprehensible alien presence; unmistakable minor supernatural activity.
-2The dead rise; a hostile ghost manifests; an apparently ordinary killer proves impossible to kill.
-3A horde of the dead; grotesque bodily transformation; a violent failure of space, time, or sorcery.
-4A vast, powerful, reality-defying entity or another direct encounter with cosmic evil.

Resolve the roll in this order:

  1. Natural 2 OR 6+ below target: lose 1 Resolve and remain frozen for the entire scene. This is a critical failure regardless of the total.

  2. Natural 12 OR 6+ above target: act normally. If current Resolve is below +3, gain 1 Resolve. A natural 12 cannot raise Resolve above 3, though other rules may do so.

  3. Total greater than 8: act normally.

  4. Total exactly 8: freeze and take no actions for one round, then act normally.

  5. Total below 8: choose either to freeze and take no actions for 1d6 rounds or to lose 1 Resolve and act normally.

Resolve cannot fall below -5. Other consequences still apply.

Repeated Horror

Each creature or phenomenon normally causes one Horror roll per character per adventure. Roll again only after substantial escalation, revelation, or new circumstances—not for remaining in the same scene.

Examples: a horde arrives (roll once), then an ally is revealed as one of them, the Horror assumes a worse form, or it returns by surprise (roll again).

Patrons and Contacts

A Contact provides one defined service within limited reach. A Patron is a powerful person or organization offering broader aid for greater obligations. Both are Connections.

Connections provide concrete access or aid, not modifiers or automatic success. Their means and interests still limit them.

New players do not start with meaningful Contacts or Patrons, but soon after play begins one or multiple may be presented.

Referee Guidance

An introductory adventure is a great time to introduce a Patron or Contact, and when the characters complete it, it is appropriate to allow characters to choose to receive the quest-giver as a Contact.

Recording Connections

Connection Slots equal 3 + Social. A Contact uses one; a Patron uses two. Acquaintances and hirelings use none. To exceed the limit, drop a Connection or take Wider Circle.

Record:

  • Name and role.

  • Reach: where or among whom they can act.

  • Service: one kind of aid for a Contact; one sphere and two example services for a Patron.

  • Interest: what they protect, want, or expect.

  • Ledger: three Favor boxes, one Debt box, and status.

An NPC becomes a Connection after the relationship is established, both sides want it to continue, and the Referee approves its Service and Reach. Money alone is insufficient.

Favor and Debt

Routine, costless aid is free. Spend Favor when a Connection spends resources, takes a risk, or uses influence:

Favor Scale Examples
1 Inconvenient Arrange an introduction, share a guarded rumor, find a buyer, provide a night’s concealment.
2 Costly or risky Hide a wanted person, obtain a scarce item, lend a specialist, compromise a minor official.
3 Grave Risk ruin or bloodshed, reveal a protected secret, mobilize armed followers, defy a powerful rival.

Within Service and Reach, Favor secures sincere cooperation without a Social throw, not automatic success. The Referee sets time, limits, and any further throws.

Without enough Favor, a Connection may provide 1-Favor aid for marked Debt. It extends no more credit until cleared. Fulfilling a called Debt clears it but earns no Favor; refusal causes Strain.

After an adventure, gain 1 Favor for advancing the Connection’s Interest, completing unpaid service, or accepting a meaningful cost. Gain 2 for saving it from ruin, captivity, or death. Maximum 3.

Network Advances

At each total earned XP threshold, choose one Network Advance. The Referee may also award one after a milestone that changes the character’s place in the world.

  • Wider Circle: Gain one Connection Slot, to a maximum of 5 + Social.

  • Trusted Ally: Broaden one Service or increase its Reach one step. Each Connection may take this once.

  • Renown: Name a region, culture, or faction and what the character is known for. Gain fitting audiences, shelter, information, and contracts, but also recognition, rivals, or obligations. Renown grants no modifier.

Advances require an established cause. Connections may otherwise be gained, changed, or lost in play.

Strained Connections

Mark Strained for ignoring an urgent plea, leaving called Debt unpaid, violating Interest, betraying confidence, or causing serious danger. Absence alone does not count.

If the result is uncertain, throw 1d6 at the end of the adventure or faction turn:

1d6 Result
1 Broken: Lost, hostile, disappeared, or dead. Cross it out.
2–3 Cold: Refuses aid until the relationship is repaired in play.
4–5 Price: Erase one Favor. If none remains, mark Debt; if Debt is already marked, the Connection goes Cold.
6 Holds: Clear Strain; the underlying trouble may remain.

Favor may secure personal intervention or temporary use of a suitable faction Asset, not control of the faction.

Reactions

Roll 2d6 for an intelligent NPC’s initial attitude; interpret results in context. Social does not modify this roll. Afterward, a character may throw Social 6+ to improve the Reaction one level.

Example: “Friendly” marauders might trade instead of attack.

2d6 Reaction
2-3 Hostile; attacks at the first opportunity. If badly outmatched, leaves and may return with allies.
4-5 Unfriendly; refuses aid and may attack if provoked.
6-8 Neutral; suspicious and unhelpful. Cooperation requires a substantial reward.
9-11 Indifferent; benign but uninterested. May share information after persuasion.
12 Friendly; helps without inducement.

Factions

Factions pursue goals independently, creating events and adventures. These rules are an abstraction, not an economic or military simulation, and also support PC-controlled factions.

The Essence of Factions

Create factions at campaign start or as needed; about 10 provides complexity without excess work. Each faction must proactively seek change and allow meaningful PC interaction. Static institutions need not be factions.

Example: A status-quo mayor is not a faction, but the thieves’ guild targeting him is. A royal spy ring may matter more than its monarch.

Faction Characteristics

Factions have Skills, Assets, and Goals.

Faction Skills

Factions use the six character Skills:

Combat: military might.

Craft: artisans and manufacturing.

Lore: research, esoteric knowledge, and sorcery.

Physical: wealth, supplies, and structures.

Social: political and public influence.

Stealth: subterfuge and espionage.

Unlisted Skills are 0. Every active faction has at least one Skill at 1. Skills range from 0 to 5.

Skill Level Meaning Example
0 Trivial Ability A sorcerer lacking military forces will have Combat 0, representing his hired guards or magical guardians.
1 Basic Competence A thieves’ guild will have Combat 1, representing its thugs who extend influence and enforce debts.
2 Good Competence A small witches’ coven will have Lore 2, representing good sorcerous skills.
3 Significant Capacity An aspiring noble courtier may have Social 3, representing her significant connections and political clout.
4 Great Power A wealthy merchant house, with control of multiple trade routes, would have Physical 4.
5 Mastery A legendary assassin’s guild will have Stealth 5, representing its ability to infiltrate any target.

Do not list Skills at 0.

Faction Assets

Assets are specific resources beyond a faction’s Skills. One relevant Asset grants +2 to a faction action. Additional Assets do not stack; they may prevent a setback or grant another fictional benefit. An Asset cannot duplicate a Skill.

Examples: a pet monster, artifact, or unique agent. A skeleton army is Combat, not an Asset; a unique Abomination of Desolation is an Asset.

Faction Goals

Give each faction at least three specific goals. Each requires 2–5 successful steps; harder goals require more.

Example: “Replace the mayor with a simulacrum” is more useful than “Take over the city.”

Sample Factions

Three example factions:

Goals:

  • Buy off the city watch (4 steps)
  • Control the Nobles’ Market (5 steps)

  • Conquer the city of Khor (5 steps)

Playing the Factions

The Referee runs one faction turn after each adventure. For faster campaigns, also run one after each in-game month.

Faction Actions

Each faction pursues one goal per turn. Roll 2d6 + relevant Skill; one relevant Asset grants +2. On 7-, suffer a setback. On 8–11, advance one step. On 12+, advance two steps.

After completing a goal, gain +1 to one Skill (maximum 5) or a new Asset, then choose a replacement goal.

Opposed Actions

When factions conflict, each rolls 2d6 + relevant Skill; one relevant Asset grants +2. The higher result wins and advances one step. The winner chooses one Skill point or Asset for the loser to lose. A tie changes nothing.

A faction with no Skill above 0 disappears.

Players and Factions

PC actions may add or remove faction Skills, Assets, and goal steps. Faction actions produce visible events and adventures. When PCs directly pursue or oppose a goal, the adventure’s outcome may replace the faction roll.

Examples: Destroying Maraella’s Abomination removes that Asset. Giving Ragbar a magical sword creates an Asset. Progress toward summoning a demon may cause strange weather, disappearances, or requests for aid.

PC Factions

PCs may found a faction from a significant resource or take over an existing faction and inherit its Skills and Assets. The players set its goals and make its faction-turn rolls. Direct adventures may replace these rolls.

Examples: A mercenary company may begin as Combat 1 with all other Skills 0. Defeating Ragbar under barbarian custom may transfer his faction to the PCs.

Identifying Magical Artifacts

Magical detection reveals that an item is magical, not its powers. Identification requires one week, 1 WP, and Lore 6+. Each retry costs the same.

Treasure is Small, Large, or a Hoard. Lesser foes have Small treasure; powerful foes, Large treasure; great monsters and rich nobles, Hoards.

Magical Artifacts

Each permanent artifact embodies a spell.

Creation requires a workshop worth 9 WP, Lore 8+, knowledge of the listed spell, and materials costing 9 WP at Tier 6, 27 WP at Tier 7, or 81 WP at Tier 8. The Referee may require rare ingredients.

Example: A Tier 6 Totem of Rahab requires Call Leviathan and 9 WP in materials. A powerful monster’s trophy may be a rare ingredient.

Artifacts are rarely sold. Characters must usually find, steal, or forge them. Place them carefully; they appear randomly only in royal or draconic Hoards. Forging one may be a campaign goal.
