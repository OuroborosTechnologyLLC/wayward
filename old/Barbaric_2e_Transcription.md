# INTRODUCTION

Barbarians! Civilized men huddle behind tall stone walls, trembling at the
thought of them. The soldiers of civilization tighten their shield walls against
the oncoming onslaught. And then they come. Savage men and women, screaming at
the top of their lungs, oblivious to fear, frothing at the mouth from rage and
lust for war. Wave after wave, they storm shields and fortifications. Many die;
the pagan gods bless their souls. But eventually, mail and castle fail. Then,
the flames of barbarism will sweep the land, leaving no stone unturned.

Or so think the civilized men. But beyond this common nightmare of cultured
folk, the reality is not as simple. Proud barbarian tribes plow fields in harsh
land and hunt for deadly game beyond the domains of kings and emperors. Their
warriors are plenty; of them, many are raiders and plunderers. But other
barbarians are merchants trading with civilization; more are guides and hunters
in far-flung provinces, and ultimately many become mercenaries to bolster the
ranks of legions crumbling under the heel of decadent rulers.

Sooner or later, many such soldiers of fortune – or, indeed, some of their
merchant and wanderer kinfolk – will see the rotten heart of civilization for
what it is: opulence and corruption, avarice, and greed. Aristocrats who send
others to kill and be killed to fulfill their whims. Merchants who would sell
their own mother for profit. Crime seeping through every crack in moribund
empires. Many will become jaded and disillusioned, discovering that the light of
civilization is as dark and cold as the steppes and forests they call home.
Others will see an opportunity.

For sometimes, what a rotten kingdom needs are a barbarian boot to kick at it:
smash its scheming nobles, lay low its selfish emperors, and bring freedom – or
new slave-masters – to the townspeople and peasants.

Sword and sorcery tales speak of cunning thieves, power-hungry sorcerers, and,
indeed, barbaric warriors. This game – Barbaric! – lets you play such heroes.
Or, more often, anti-heroes. Pit your sword-arm and knowledge of eldritch
secrets against ravaging bandits, scheming nobles, diabolic demons, mighty
dragons – and, ultimately, the rotten and ugly face of moribund civilization
itself.

So, pour your beer, devour your pizza, turn up the volume of your favorite heavy
metal album – and prepare to become… **Barbaric!**

# BASIC RULES AND CHARACTERS

This chapter is the heart of Barbaric! In it, we present the simple rules for
creating and equipping characters and resolving the actions those characters
attempt.

## DIE ROLL CONVENTIONS

Barbaric! uses common six-sided dice. We note each die you need to roll as a
“d6”. For example, “2d6” means “roll two six-sided dice and add the results
together”.

In this game, a “modifier” is a number you add or subtract from a dice roll. For
example, “2d6, modifier -2” means “roll two dice, add the results together, and
then subtract 2 from the total”. This is often written as “2d6-2”.

D66 is a special die roll, in which you throw two dice, and count one as the
“ones” and one as the “tens”. This will produce 36 results from 11 to 66. Just
be sure to pick which die is the “tens” and which is the “ones” before rolling
the dice.

## THE BASIC MECHANIC

**Throw 2d6**: throw two six-sided dice and add the results together, add the
relevant skill level as a modifier, and compare to a predetermined difficulty
number. If the total equals or exceeds the difficulty number, you succeed.
Otherwise, you fail.

**Skill notation**: "Throw Skill 6+" means throw two dice, add them together and
add the relevant skill, and if the total equals or exceeds 6, you succeed. For
example, a Referee might ask a player to throw Lore 8+ to determine the kind of
disease ravaging a trade caravan. The player would throw 2d6, add the Lore skill
level, and succeed on a result of 8 or more.

An unmodified result of 2 is always a failure – potentially a critical fumble.
An unmodified, “natural” result of 12 is always a success – and a critical one
at that!

Referees should call for skill throws whenever a situation is uncertain and
success or failure will produce interesting results. For example, there is no
need to have players make skill throws for routine tasks performed in routine
circumstances. Even a task with an Easy (4+) Difficulty Number should only be
thrown if the results are uncertain and dramatic.

Note that barbarians are hardy and resourceful people. If your character has no
points in a skill, treat it as “Skill 0”, and simply roll 2d6 on its checks,
without any modifiers. There is no need to note this on your character sheet or
monster description: when a skill is absent, simply assume that it is at 0.

## SKILLS

There are 6 skills: Combat, Craft, Lore, Physical, Social, and Stealth.

**Combat**: this skill covers actions that involve committing violence. Anything
from spears to catapults.

**Craft**: this skill covers all crafting and mechanical tasks, from repairing a
boat to building a house. It also covers general knowledge and wilderness
survival skills.

**Lore**: this skill covers knowledge acquired by a formal education, like
medicine, history, or alchemy. This skill also covers the occult and eldritch
lore, including casting spells.

**Physical**: any time a character needs to exert themselves, use this skill.
This can include feats of athleticism or resisting harmful effects such as
poisons.

**Social**: useful whenever a character wishes to interact with other beings in
a social manner. Use this skill to bargain, lie, carouse, or conduct
negotiations.

**Stealth**: this skill is useful for any acts of subterfuge, such as sneaking
or pickpocketing. It also covers acts of subterfuge such as picking a lock.

## CHARACTER CREATION

1. Think about what kind of character you want to play in Barbaric! Do you want
   to play a tough barbarian, a dastardly sorcerer, a cunning thief - or
   something completely different?

2. Distribute 5 points among your six **Skills**. You may not assign more than 3
   points to any single skill.

3. Pick an **Archetype** from the next chapter. This represents your specific
   profession and training, above and beyond common skills.

4. Your character’s **Stamina**, representing your resistance to injury, equals
   14 plus three times your Physical skill.

5. Your **Defense** rating, representing your ability to dodge or parry incoming
   attacks, is equal to 8 + half your Combat skill (rounded up). For example, a
   character with Combat 3 will have a Defense rating of 10+.

6. You start the game with food and water for 3 days, a backpack, a waterskin, a
   knife, and 3d6x10 gold coins worth of items, purchased from the equipment
   lists in the **Equipment** chapter.

7. If you have the Lore skill at level 1 or higher, you gain 1 **Spell** of your
   choice per Lore skill level.

8. Finalize your character, choosing a **name** and an **appearance**.

9. Make preliminary notes on your character’s **motivation** and
   **personality**.

## FATIGUE

When a character is Fatigued, they suffer a -1 penalty to all actions and may
only move 10m per combat round, even when not attacking. Fatigue is caused by
various conditions, such as starvation or arduous marches, and ihas no
connection to Stamina.

## ADVANTAGE

Having Advantage when throwing dice means throwing three dice and picking the
highest two among them rather than simply throwing 2d6. Advantage is often
applied to rolls by non-humans and in Archetypes, as well as in spells.

## NON-HUMANS

Some settings permit non-human characters. Unlike humans, non-humans only have 4
points to distribute between their skills in character generation rather than 5.
However, they have their own special abilities.

**Antediluvians**: sorcerous humanoids predating extant civilization by
millennia. They can sense magic instinctively within a 30m radius, without
requiring a throw (see the Sorcery chapter for details). They also gain
Advantage on Reaction throws when encountering demons, sentient undead, and
other Chaotic beings.

**Apefolk**: Apes uplifted by sorcery or super-science to full sentience. They
gain Advantage on any feat of brute strength and +3 to their Stamina score.

**Dwarf**: The classic race of fantasy and Norse mythology: short and sturdy
denizens of subterranean mountain realms. Dwarves are often skilled smiths and
stonemasons. They gain Advantage to resist poison and disease. In underground or
mountainous locations, may spot secret doors, traps, and abnormal construction
on a Craft 6+ throw.

**Elf**: The classic race of fantasy and Norse mythology. These forest
spirit-folk gain Advantage on Stealth throws in forests. They also may see in
dim light as in daylight and in darkness as in dim light.

**Gecko-Kin**: Humanoids evolved or uplifted from geckos. They can climb walls
and ceilings as easily as walking on the ground. They also may see in dim light
as in daylight and in darkness as in dim light.

**Lizard-Kin**: Humanoids evolved or uplifted from water-dwelling lizards. Their
unarmed damage is 2d6, and they have a natural armor rating of 2. Lizard-Kin can
hold breath for 10 minutes.

# ARCHETYPES

Archetypes describe your character’s specialist and professional training. The
Referee should assume that the character is knowledgeable in this field, in
addition to the special abilities listed below. For example, a Priest will be
knowledgeable about religion, and a Hunter will know how to set up traps and
track animals. This is open to Referee discretion, but we urge the Referee to be
fair, generous, and reasonable about such matters.

**Fencer**: whenever the character has a natural 12 result on a melee attack
throw or beats the target number to hit by 6 or more, halve the target’s armor
(rounding down), in addition to applying maximum damage for a critical hit, as
normal.

**Beast Master**: gain Advantage when identifying flora or fauna, as well as on
Reaction throws when encountering non-magical animals.

**Berserker**: when losing Stamina but still conscious, the character may choose
to enter a Rage. This grants the berserker Advantage to hit and a +4 bonus to
damage in melee combat, but also grants the character's enemies Advantage to hit
them. The Rage lasts until the end of that combat encounter.

**Burglar**: gain Advantage on Stealth rolls when lockpicking or attempting to
bypass or disarm traps.

**Explorer**: gain Advantage on throws to avoid getting lost while traveling
overland.

**Healer**: gain Advantage when performing Triage on a Wounded character.

**Hunter**: gain Advantage on Surprise throws when attacking from ambush.

**Knight**: ignore the movement penalty for wearing Heavy Armor.

**Mariner**: gain Advantage to throws to avoid getting lost while travelling on
the open seas.

**Merchant**: gain Advantage when attempting to buy or sell goods, or when
negotiating any sort of contract or deal.

**Priest**: gain Advantage on Reaction rolls when encountering Lawful characters
and creatures. If you have any followers, they gain a +1 bonus to their Morale.

**Professional**: you have Advantage on throws related to a non-combat
profession (such as an advocate, chef, or herald). This never applies in combat
situations or to spellcasting.

**Pugilist**: you cause 2d6 damage (rather than 1d6) with your unarmed attacks.

<!-- Source PDF page 13 -->

**Runner**: you move an additional 1d6m per combat round rather than just 10m;
if you also move instead of acting (e.g., instead of attacking), you move 30m in
total that round.

**Sage**: gain an Advantage in one field of knowledge, such as astrology,
history, or mathematics.

**Shaman**: you may perform a Divine Audience (per the spell) once a week
without requiring a Lore throw or risking a mishap.

**Sniper**: whenever the character has a natural 12 result on a ranged attack
throw or beats the target number to hit by 6 or more, halve the target’s armor
(rounding down), in addition to applying maximum damage for a critical hit, as
normal.

**Sorcerer**: gain 3 spells of your choice.

**Thief**: gain Advantage on rolls for pickpocketing or palming an item,
performing sleight-of-hand, or when fencing stolen goods.

**Warrior**: gain 2 Combat Moves beyond those you would normally receive for
your Combat skill.

# COMBAT MOVES

Characters receive a Combat Move for every level of the Combat skill above 1.
For example, a character with Combat 3 receives 2 Combat Moves. These must be
selected from the list of Combat Moves available for the character’s Combat
skill.

### COMBAT 2

**Archery**: The character is proficient in bows. Increase the range of any bow
used by the character by 50%. Furthermore, when not in melee, but within 10m of
the target, character gains a +1 bonus to attack and +2 to damage.

**Armored Combat**: The character is proficient with heavy armor and gains +2 to
armor protection when wearing heavy armor.

**Polearms**: The character is proficient in polearms and staffs, and
automatically wins initiative over enemies with shorter weapons.

**Single Weapon**: The character has mastered an agile fighting style with
one-handed weapons and one free hand, gaining a +1 bonus to attack with
one-handed weapons and +1 armor protection when wielding a single weapon. This
bonus does not apply when using shields.

**Throwing Weapon**: The character can throw weapons with speed and accuracy,
gaining an additional attack with one-handed, small, thrown weapons every other
round.

**Two-Handed Weapon**: The character is proficient with two-handed swords, axes,
and mauls and gains +3 to damage with these weapons.

**Weapon and Shield**: When using a one-handed weapon with a shield, the
character gains a +1 bonus to attack.

### COMBAT 3

**Smite**: The character is skilled in delivering wild, powerful blows. By
applying a penalty to hit, they gain the same bonus as damage. Characters may
incur up to a -4 penalty to hit and gain +1 damage per point of penalty.

**Mounted Combat**: The character is an expert in attacking from a mount’s back.
While mounted, the character gains Advantage to hit targets on foot.

**Shield Bash**: The character can strike an enemy with a shield, albeit at a -2
to hit penalty.

<!-- Source PDF page 15 -->

### COMBAT 4

**Defend Others**: The character can intercept blows meant to hit other
characters. If the defender can move up to 10 meters and then protects an
adjacent (2m away) character from incoming attacks using the defender’s own
defense and armor. The defender can attack normally.

**Disarming**: The character can disarm enemies by striking their weapons. They
must successfully attack these enemies with a -2 penalty to hit. The weapon
lands 1d6 meters from the enemy, requiring the enemy to move and expend an
action to retrieve it.

**Fearsome Charge**: The character is a master of mounted combat and inflicts
double damage on successful hits when using melee weapons from a mount’s back.
This requires the mounted character to move prior to attacking.

**Two Weapon Fighting**: The character may wield two weapons with skill and
power, gaining two attacks per round with -1 penalty to hit with their primary
hand weapon and a -2 to hit penalty to the secondary hand weapon.

### COMBAT 5

**Whirlwind**: The hero moves as fast as a tiger and attacks as fiercely as a
cobra. They are a whirlwind among the wheat and leave a trail of bloody bodies
in their wake. The character gains an extra attack. If the character fights with
two weapons, then they have three attacks per round, though two-weapon fighting
penalties apply.

**Stronghold**: The character stands their ground and will not fall back. Their
shield is like a castle wall, their blade like a stockade. They are a stronghold
stopping the advance of entire enemy armies! Enemies suffer a -1 penalty to hit
character, and once per round the character may automatically parry one attack,
except for massive attacks such as dragon claws, which, at the Referee’s
discretion, may be impossible for human-sized creatures to parry.

# EXPERIENCE

Heroic and determined characters may develop their talents quickly and become
living legends. To represent this, Barbaric! offers an experience system.

Characters gain 1 XP per adventure. Every 5 XP, the character rises in Rank. The
Ranks are:

1. Experienced (you start here)

2. Expert

3. Master

4. Grandmaster

5. Legend

**When they gain a new Rank, they may choose one of the following:**

- Gain one skill point to add to any skill; you may not raise any skill beyond
  5.

- Gain one Combat Move, if your Combat skill is 2 or greater.

- Gain +4 Stamina.

- Gain 2 new spells.

- Once per lifetime, a character may gain a second Archetype as a Rank increase.

A player character may increase their Rank up to four times (up to Legend). Most
NPCs do not rise above the Expert level; higher Ranked NPCs are rare and unique.

### OPTIONAL RULE – RAPID ADVANCEMENT

If the Referee wishes to allow the characters to reach legendary levels of
proficiency within a short campaign, they may grant 1 XP per session rather than
per adventure.

# HERO POINTS

Heroic barbarians and dastardly sorcerers sometimes survive ordeals that lesser
mortals perish in.

Each player starts each game session with 2 personal Hero Points. The group also
has an additional number of Hero Points equal to the number of player characters
present that session.

All pools reset at the beginning of each session.

A character may use their personal Hero Points, and with the other players'
consent, points from the group pool.

**By spending Hero Points, a character may:**

- Reroll any single die throw (including damage). They may take the better
  result.

- Ask the Referee to reroll a single NPC's die throw (including damage). The
  second result stands.

- Reroll a Triage roll or a Sorcery mishap. They may take the better result.

## OPTIONAL RULE: ENCUMBRANCE

For a more hardcore adventuring experience, limit the items a character may
carry to a number equal to 10 + twice their Physical skill. Tiny items such as a
needle, as well as clothes and backpacks, do not count as items; 20 arrows or 6
torches count as one item; a heavy weapon such as a great sword or medium armor
count as two items; and heavy armor counts as 3 items. Carrying more items than
a character’s encumbrance limit halves the character’s movement rate. Characters
can carry a maximum of items equal to twice their encumbrance limit.

# COMBAT

The life of a barbarian is fraught with peril, but by sword and zeal, their foes
may fall! The following rules adjudicate personal combat.

**Surprise**: When two potentially hostile parties encounter one another, there
is a chance that either group will be surprised. Each party’s character with the
lowest Stealth skill throws Stealth 6+. If one party succeeds and the other
fails, the successful side surprises the other and enjoys one free round of
action. If both parties succeed or fail, there is no surprise. Apply modifier +3
to the throw for any party setting up an ambush, and a -1 penalty to the throw
for any party that has vehicles or that is a group of over 10 creatures.

**Initiative**: at the beginning of combat, each character throws 2d6 and adds
their Combat skill for their Initiative score. Characters act in order from the
highest to the lowest Initiative.

**Time and movement**: a combat round represents 6 seconds of in-game time.
During which, characters may move up to 10m and attack, or move up to 20m
without attacking.

**Attacking**: throw Combat (varied target number, see below) to hit a target in
melee or within Effective range; if the result is equal or higher than the
target’s Defense rating, the attack hits. Otherwise, it misses. Hitting targets
beyond Effective range incurs modifier -2 .

**FRENZY**! If a character puts their enemy “out of combat” in melee, they may
move up to 2m and attack another foe They may do so a number of times equal to
their Combat skill per combat round.

**Damage**: Upon a hit, throw the weapon's damage dice, subtract any armor, and
apply the result to the target's Stamina. If Stamina is positive, the character
is not wounded, only bruised. However, if Stamina runs out, the character is
Wounded and typically is out of combat. Characters may attempt to keep fighting
even when Wounded; throw Physical 6+ to keep fighting when receiving a Wound.

Any further damage dealt to a Wounded character causes additional Wounds. The
character may throw Physical 6+ to keep fighting every time they receive a
Wound. However, every wound the character suffers incurs modifier -1 on the
subsequent Triage roll, as well as to the roll to keep fighting when wounded.

Note that if a single attack causes more damage than twice the target’s maximum
Stamina, the target dies immediately.

<!-- Source PDF page 23 -->

**Critical Hits**: on a natural attack throw of 12, or if the attacker exceeds
the target’s Defense rating (which is 8 + half the target’s Combat skill,
rounded up) by 6 or more, the attack causes maximum damage. Additional effects
described in the tables at the end of this section might apply as well. Natural
attack rolls of 12 always hit, regardless of the target’s Defense rating.

**Healing**: When combat is over, characters with a positive Stamina recover
their full Stamina within 10 minutes of catching their breath and resting. After
combat, each Wounded character, conscious or not, must roll once on the Triage
table after combat. Apply modifier -1 per Wound the character suffers; apply an
additional modifier -2 to the roll if the character is attempting to recover on
their own without first aid.

**Morale**: monsters and NPCs rarely fight to the death. Referees should
consider rolling a Morale check for monsters and NPCs after they suffer their
group’s first death, when they are surprised, after they take more than 50%
casualties, and whenever NPCs suffer unduly in combat. Both sides throw 2d6 and
add their leader’s Social skill; apply a modifier -2 if the NPCs’ leader has
been killed. If one side’s result is greater than or equal to two times the
other side’s result, the losing side of the Morale check will flee. Combatants
get a free attack against fleeing opponents. PCs do not roll for Morale; NPCs in
their service however, do.

# EXPLORATION

Adventurers often embark on journeys of exploration, be they tomb-robbing or
charting unknown lands. This chapter provides rules for such adventures.

## OVERLAND MAP AND MOVEMENT

The typical wilderness adventure map uses a 10km (6 mile) hexagon grid. A
character may travel 30km (3 hexes) a day on foot on open plains. Reduce this by
half (15km or1.5 hexes per day) if the character is encumbered or wearing heavy
armor.

Different terrain types will further slow the rate of travel as noted below. For
the sake of simplicity, terrain modifiers do not stack; choose the most
appropriate terrain type for the current region:

```text
                          Terrain Movement Effects

Terrain                               Movement Rate Multiplier

City/Urban                            2/3

Desert/Badlands                       2/3

Forest/Jungle                         2/3

Hills/Mountains                       1/3

Plains/Grasslands                     Full

Swamp/Aquatic                         1/3
```

A **trail** allows characters on foot or mounted (but not vehicles) to move at
their full movement speed regardless of terrain. A **road** allows vehicles to
travel at full speed.

### FORCED MARCHING

A character may double their overland travel range by making a forced march for
one day. At the end of that day, they must throw Physical 4+ or be Fatigued.
This is subject to a further modifier -1 per further day of forced marching.
Fatigued characters may not make a forced march.

<!-- Source PDF page 29 -->

### BECOMING LOST

It is easy to lose one’s way and get lost in the deep wilderness. A party
following a trail, road, coast, or waterway will never get lost. In all other
instances of travel, make a daily Craft throw using the target numbers given in
the table below to avoid getting lost. Remember that a result of 2 on the dice
before modifiers is always a failure regardless of skill. Apply modifier -1 for
bad weather conditions and modifier -2 to travel in darkness.

```text
                                    Getting Lost
    Terrain                               Craft throw to avoid getting lost
    City/Urban                            4+
    Desert/Badlands                       6+
    Forest/Jungle                         6+
    Hills/Mountains                       4+
    Open Seas                             6+
    Plains/Grasslands                     3+
    Swamps/Aquatic                        5+
```

## CAMPING

Selecting a good site to camp at night can save lives. When making camp, the
Referee may allow the party to choose one skill throw for the party to create a
defensible camp and avoid random encounters at night. This is usually a Craft 6+
throw in wilderness locations. The Referee may allow a Physical 6+ throw when it
is possible to climb to a safe and otherwise inaccessible location.

Characters should keep watches at night. Only characters on watch will be alert
and fully armored if trouble arises; the rest will be asleep in normal clothes.
However, each character must sleep at least half a night to enjoy the benefits
of rest (such as healing) and to avoid being Fatigued.

<!-- Source PDF page 30 -->

## FORAGING

Characters low on food or water must forage to survive. Foraging characters move
at 2/3 speed, which is cumulative with terrain effects, and may not attempt any
forced marches. The character with the highest Craft skill in the party throws
Craft 6+ to find enough food and water for the party for that day. Add modifier
+1 per two additional foraging group members, to a maximum of total modifier +3
. Referees should make the roll more difficult depending on the terrain: it will
be more difficult to find food and water in the desert than in a bucolic forest.

Going over 2 days without food causes Fatigue; characters going over one week
without food are penalized by modifier -2 to all actions; after 4 weeks without
food, the character dies.

## UNDERGROUND MOVEMENT

Often the Referee will use a square grid map to represent a ruin or dungeon the
players are exploring. The scale is typically 3m (or 10ft) per square, but other
scales are possible.

The time unit used while exploring underground ruins is 10-minute turns, equal
to 100 combat rounds each. While exploring, characters are moving slowly and
carefully on alert for hazards and hidden features. Thus, an exploring character
will usually explore at a rate of 120m per 10-minute turn.

Because of the stress inherent to carefully exploring and moving around in an
underground environment, characters will have to rest for 1 turn after every 50
minutes of exploration; otherwise, they will be Fatigued.

### LIGHT AND DARKNESS

Subterranean ruins and moonless nights rarely contain ambient light. Characters
must use their own light sources to be able to see anything. Torches are a
staple, andthey dimly illuminate a 10m radius around their bearer. In dim light,
characters suffer modifier -1 to all actions requiring sight, including hitting
targets in combat; in full darkness, this increases to modifier -2.

# SORCERY!

Sorcerers wield mighty spells against their foes, warping reality at their whim.
But every time they dabble in the arcane, they put their souls in peril. This
chapter discusses sorcery, its spells, and its risks.

### READING AND SENSING MAGIC

Characters with the Lore skill may sense or read magical inscriptions or detect
nearby magic on a Lore 8+ throw.

### LEARNING NEW SPELLS

Any character may attempt to learn a spell from an eldritch tome. Studying a
spell takes a week and requires a Lore 8+ throw. The character may re-attempt
this throw, which takes one week per attempt. A result of 2 before modifiers
will destroy the specific spell formula (but not the entire tome). Even
characters with Lore 0 may learn spells. Note that a natural throw of 2 when
learning a spell, or a modified result of 4 or less, causes a Mishap, just like
when casting a spell.

## SPELLCASTING

Any character, regardless of skill, may attempt to cast a spell they know. There
is no hard limit to the number of spells a character may cast in a day. However,
there are two main limiting factors to a sorcerer's spellcasting ambition:
casting time and the risk of mishaps.

Casting a spell is always a Lore 8+ roll.

Each spell has a range, noted in parentheses after its name. For example,
Respite (Range: touch) means that it requires touching the spell’s target.

Casting a spell normally takes 10 minutes.

A sorcerer may cast a spell hastily within one combat round, albeit at modifier
-2 to the Lore roll.

A result of double sixes on 2d6 is always a success, regardless of skill or
spell difficulty.

<!-- Source PDF page 33 -->

On a success, the spell is cast at the end of the 10-minute turn (or round in
the case of hasty casting). The spell's effect will take place after all other
actions in that combat round.

### CRITICAL FAILURES

A spell critically fails if the sorcerer throws a natural 2 on the spellcasting
throw, or the sorcerer fails the Lore throw by 4 or more. In either such case,
the spell fails automatically, and the sorcerer throws on the Spellcasting
Mishap table below.

## MONSTER FORMAT

**Monsters have their statistics listed as follows:**

Stamina, Movement, Armor, an attack, skills, and special abilities.

**For example**: *A Giant Gecko has the following stats: Stamina 16, Defense 9+,
Move 10m, Armor 2, Bite (melee 2d6 piercing). Combat 2, Physical 2.
Wall-Walking.*

This means that this horrible lizard has 16 Stamina, requires a roll of 9+ to be
hit, moves 10m/round, has 2 points of armor, attacks with a bite at modifier +2
to hit that causes 2d6 piercing damage, and can walk on walls.

Note that, like any other character, a monster can move in lieu of attacking,
allowing double movement that round.

Also note that monsters may have multiple attacks per round, as noted in their
descriptions.

## SPECIAL ABILITIES

**Disease**: after taking damage from a diseased creature, the victim must throw
Physical 6+ or develop symptoms within 1 hour. A disease’s effect is at the
Referee’s discretion but can range from a -1 penalty to all actions to losing
1d6 Stamina per day (without being able to regenerate them until the disease
passes or is cured). The victim may throw Physical 6+ each subsequent day to
overcome the disease.

**Fear**: any mortal who beholds the monstrosity must throw Lore 6+ or flee (or
cower) in fear for 1d6 rounds.

**Incorporeal**: immune to non-silver, non-magical weapons.

**Paralysis**: upon a successful attack, the victim must throw Physical 6+ or be
paralyzed for the next 1d6x10 minutes. No matter how many attacks the victim
suffers from a paralyzing creature that round, they only need to throw Physical
6+ once per round to avoid paralysis.

**Stun**: throw Physical 6+ to avoid getting stunned for 1d6 rounds. A Stunned
character may not move or act but is not helpless. Creatures attacking a Stunned
character attack with Advantage.

**Undead**: immune to poison, disease, suffocation, and mind-affecting sorcery.

# FACTIONS

A good campaign setting is not static. Various forces act independently, both
covertly and overtly, to pursue goals, sometimes beyond the reach of the player
characters. Not everything revolves around the player characters after all, and
events are bound to happen both as consequences of their actions, and completely
independently of them. To facilitate the Referee’s job of running such a living,
breathing world, we present simple rules for running factions.

Note that these rules do not constitute a proper mechanical, economic, or
military simulation of actions and events, but rather an abstraction capable of
easily producing plot hooks, background events, and matters for the player
characters to meddle in. They are intended to represent a mapping of actors and
events onto actual gameplay. This system also allows for running PC-controlled
factions, though again in an abstract manner avoiding excessive use of
spreadsheets.

## THE ESSENCE OF FACTIONS

At the beginning of the campaign, or whenever in-game activity or the Referee’s
imagination calls for it, the Referee may design factions. The recommended
number of factions is 10 per campaign, allowing for some interesting complexity
while still limiting the Referee’s workload.

Factions should always be proactive elements trying to change the status quo,
with whom the PCs can interact in a meaningful way. Not every political entity
in the game needs a faction. The local mayor, who wants to run things as they
always have been, would not be a faction. Instead, that mayor would be a
potential target for factions like thieves’ guilds, dastardly sorcerers, and
aspiring conquerors. The same might often apply to monarchies: the court
sorcerer’s machinations or the royal spy ring would potentially be of greater
interest to the game as factions than the titular ruler himself.

## FACTION CHARACTERISTICS

Factions have Skills, Assets, and Goals.

### FACTION SKILLS

Each faction has six skills, parallel to the ones possessed by Barbaric!
characters:

**Combat**: military might.

**Craft**: access to artisans and manufacturing capabilities.

**Lore**: access to esoteric knowledge, research abilities, and sorcerous
prowess.

**Physical**: economic capability, supplies, and physical structures such as
castles and warehouses.

**Social**: political influence and control of public opinion.

**Stealth**: subterfuge and espionage capabilities.

All factions have at least 0 in each Skill, representing the basic capabilities
of a significant force in the setting. However, all active factions have at
least one Skill at level 1. Skills range from 1 to 5. Level 1 represents basic
but still significant capabilities, while level 5 would represent vast power and
resources in the given field. Use the examples on the following table as
guidelines:

```text

    Skill Level     Meaning         Example
                                    A sorcerer lacking military forces will have
    0               Trivial Ability Combat 0, representing his hired guards or
                                    magical guardians.
                    Basic           A thieves’ guild will have Combat 1,
    1
                    Competence      representing its thugs.
                    Good            A small witches’ coven will have Lore 2,
    2
                    Competence      representing good sorcerous skills.
                                    An aspiring noble courtier may have Social 3,
                    Significant
    3                               representing her significant connections and
                    Capacity
                                    political clout.
                                    A wealthy merchant house, with control of
    4               Great Power
                                    multiple trade routes, would have Physical 4.
                                    A legendary assassin’s guild will have Stealth 5,
    5               Mastery
                                    representing its ability to infiltrate any target.
```

When writing a faction’s Skills, there is no need to list skills at 0; just
assume that any skill not written down is at 0.

## FACTION ASSETS

Assets are specific resources the faction possesses, above and beyond its
general capabilities represented by their Skills. This includes pet monsters,
magical artifacts, unique and noteworthy individuals in the faction’s employ,
and so on.

When rolling for a faction’s action (see below), if a relevant Asset exists, the
faction gains an Advantage.

Assets are freeform and at the Referee’s discretion. However, once again,
anything already directly represented by Skills shouldn’t be an asset. A
necromancer’s skeleton army is not a good asset, it is represented by the
faction's Combat skill. However, an Abomination of Desolation, permitting the
necromancer to wield grand eldritch might, would indeed be an asset.

## FACTION GOALS

Each faction has at least three goals. A goal is a concrete purpose the faction
wants to achieve. Goals should be specific: “taking over the city” is a less
effective goal than “replacing the mayor with a simulacrum”. This allows for
better interaction between the PCs and the faction’s machinations, and raises
more concrete plot hooks.

A goal has 2 to 5 steps, representing the number of successful actions the
faction requires to reach that goal. The harder the goal, the more steps it
requires to achieve.

## PLAYING THE FACTIONS

By default, the Referee plays the factions to quickly generate background events
and plot hooks. This takes place in faction turns. Each faction turn is of
indeterminate length, usually around one month, but typically one faction turn
occurs after each adventure, or, for a more intense experience, after each
adventure or in-game month (Referee’s discretion).

### FACTION ACTIONS

In each faction turn, each faction chooses to pursue one of its goals. The
Referee can vary this, as factions should have multiple facets to their
activities. To advance a goal, roll 2d6 + the relevant faction’s skill. If the
faction has an asset relevant for that action, it gains an Advantage on that
roll. A total of 7 or less is a setback - the faction will have to try again in
a later turn. A total of 8-11 advances the faction towards its goal by one step.
A total of 12 or greater advances the faction towards its goal by two steps.

When a faction achieves a goal, it gains either one point in a skill of its
choosing (to a maximum of 5), or a new Asset. It then sets a new goal to replace
the former one; factions are always looking for more power and goals to pursue.

<!-- Source PDF page 55 -->

### OPPOSED ACTIONS

If a faction’s action conflicts with another faction’s interests, each faction
rolls 2d6 + their relevant skill, with Assets granting Advantages as above. The
higher total wins. The victor advances its goal by one step; the loser loses 1
skill point or 1 asset (victor’s choice!). A draw means a stalemate, where no
faction gains or loses anything.

A faction with no skills remaining above 0 fades away into obscurity. But who
knows, it might return later for revenge!

## PLAYERS AND FACTIONS

Players may influence factions in various ways.

The Referee should allow actions by PCs to affect factions, and vice versa. For
example, if the PCs destroy Maraella the Sorceress’ Abomination of Desolation
during an adventure, obviously she will lose that Asset. If a PC bestows his
mighty magical sword to Chief Ragbar (unlikely, we concede), this becomes
Ragbar’s Asset. On the other hand, of course, faction actions are apparent in
the game world; there may be weird weather, or rumors of kidnapped sacrificial
victims, when Maraella advances towards summoning the Cacodemon, and players
might be hired by Khor’s mayor to provide him with security against the Guild’s
machinations. If the Referee puts the PCs in the direct path of a faction (and
why not?) then the PCs can also be directly responsible for advancing a faction
closer towards its goal, or even inflicting a setback. Referees should determine
what exactly would be the faction game outcome for a group’s adventure.

### PC FACTIONS

There are two ways PCs may end up controlling a faction: by establishing a new
faction or by taking over an existing one. PCs who acquire a significant
resource may attempt to establish a faction using it. For example, PCs who
gather a mercenary company can become a faction with Combat 1, and 0 in the
other skills, which will grow in skill as the mercenary company gains notoriety
and might. On the other hand, PCs may take over an existing faction, for example
defeating Ragbar in honorable one-on-one combat, thus gaining - by barbarian
custom - his position as warlord. In such a case, the PCs inherit all the
original faction’s Skills and Assets.

When PCs run a faction, they establish its goals, then make its rolls every
faction turn, choosing which goal to pursue. The adventures they embark on would
likely be directly related to their goals. Referees might replace the faction
action rolls whenever the PCs get directly involved in the action itself.

# TREASURE

In Swords and Sorcery tales, adventurers pursue treasure. This chapter presents
simple rules for generating treasures belonging to diabolical monsters, found in
barbarian hoards, or in long-forgotten ruins.

### IDENTIFYING MAGICAL ARTIFACTS

Simple sorcerous magical detection will ascertain that the item is magical, but
only deep experimentation will reveal its secrets. Identifying a magical
artifact’s capabilities requires a week of research, 100gp in reagents and
research equipment, and a Lore 6+ throw. The sorcerer can make as many attempts
as they like, but each attempt takes a week and costs 100gp.

## GENERATING TREASURES

There are three types of treasures: Small, Large, and Hoard. Bandits and lesser
sentient monsters often gather a Small treasure. Powerful monsters usually
gather a Large treasure. Only the most powerful monsters, as well as rich
nobles, possess Hoards.

When generating a treasure, note that the magic item column usually gives a
throw on 2d6 necessary for magic items to be present in the treasure. In
Barbaric! all treasures are worth at least 1000 gold coins.

```text

               Coins and Jewelry
Treasure Type  (in 1000s of gold  Magical Artifacts and Alchemical Potions
               coins)
Small          1d6                Any 1 potion on a throw of 8+.
Large          3d6                2d3 potions.
Hoard          6d6                2d3 potions; 1d6 magical artifacts.
```

## MAGICAL ARTIFACTS

The mightiest magic lies in magical artifacts, permanently enchanted items of
great power. Each such artifact relies on a spell and embodies it in material
form.

Creating artifacts requires a workshop worth at least 10,000 gold coins. The
Referee may also decide that special and rare ingredients, such as trophies
taken from rare and powerful monsters, are required to craft magical artifacts.
Once the artifact crafter has set up their workshop and gathered the required
trophies, they will need gold, and the required magic spell. Creating an
artifact requires a Lore 8+ throw, the knowledge of a spell (as noted in the
item description), and materials (their cost is noted in the artifact
description. For example, Totem of Rahab (Call Leviathan, 12,000 gold coins)
requires the Call Leviathan spell to forge, and costs 12,000 in gold coins to
create.

Note that magical items are only very rarely available for sale; characters
desiring such treasure must either find it, steal it, or forge it themselves.

**A note to the Referee**: magical artifacts are powerful and rare. They should
be placed with care in the campaign setting and are only randomly found within
the hoards of kings and dragons. Forging a magical artifact can be a major
campaign goal for sorcerers, shamans, and priests.
